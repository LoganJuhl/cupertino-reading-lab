# Note to Alexis

Unsent draft, updated 2026-10-04. Approval status: [UPSTREAM.md](../UPSTREAM.md#status).

This theme is based on the outstanding Cupertino theme created by Alexis C. Cupertino provides much of the visual foundation and native-feeling interface that made this project possible. This project extends that foundation with a different focus: long-form reading and document consumption in Obsidian.

Differences, notices, and test limits: [FEATURE-COMPARISON.md](FEATURE-COMPARISON.md), [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md), [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md). Previews: [light](../screenshot.png), [dark](../screenshot-dark.png). They are macOS captures of a synthetic note and predate the October width-fallback edit.

The draft requests permission for Community submission. Also confirm the original sources and any additional notices for inherited checkbox, callout, and platform-control SVGs.

Hi Alexis,

I've been working on Cupertino Reading Lab v4, an Obsidian theme built on top of Cupertino, and I'd like to share it with you for review.

Thank you for creating Cupertino. Its design and polish are a major part of why I chose it as the foundation. My version keeps much of that interface and focuses on long-form reading: bundled Newsreader, a consistent prose measure, heading hierarchy, quieter callouts and tables, and contained scrolling for wide equations and diagrams. Most navigation and mobile interface behavior remains your work.

Cupertino and your contribution are prominently credited in the README and upstream record. The original copyright and MIT license notices remain intact, including inside the distributed CSS. The repository also documents testing limits and remaining asset-provenance questions.

Review copy: [Cupertino Reading Lab](https://github.com/LoganJuhl/cupertino-reading-lab).

I'd eventually like to submit this derivative to the Obsidian Community directory. Its current fork policy requires publicly verifiable written approval from the original author. Would you be comfortable with me maintaining and submitting it? If so, I'm happy to record your approval wherever you prefer it to be publicly verifiable.

Thank you again for Cupertino and for making it available as open source.

Logan
